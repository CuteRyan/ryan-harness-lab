# Social Perception Research

Compare how the same subject appears in different markets without flattening local language,
platform culture, or source type into one global impression.

## Workflow

1. Define the subject, markets, time window, platforms, and business question.
2. Build a query set for each market: official name, local-script spelling, common aliases,
   campaign names, member names, and relevant local-language terms.
3. Read [platform-routing.md](references/platform-routing.md), inspect the current environment,
   and use existing approved connections before declaring a platform unavailable.
4. Collect visual and textual evidence separately for every market. Include official accounts,
   media or brand accounts, and audience communities when available.
5. Analyze each market independently before comparing them.
6. Report the conclusion first, then evidence, differences, opportunities, risks, and gaps.

## Image Analysis

- Inspect the image or video frame itself. Do not infer a visual pattern from captions alone.
- Record recurring colors, styling, settings, poses, editing formats, member emphasis, objects,
  cultural references, memes, and brand or category associations.
- Separate official positioning from media framing, fan edits, and critical or mocking imagery.
- Link representative examples. Do not bulk-copy copyrighted media into the deliverable.
- Treat a pattern as tentative when it depends on one account, event, or campaign.

## Keyword Analysis

- Preserve the original-language keyword and add a plain Korean translation.
- Record who used it, on which platform, in what context, and with what apparent meaning.
- Separate self-description, media language, fan vocabulary, search terms, and criticism.
- Do not convert visible engagement counts into market-wide popularity or sentiment claims.
- Quantify only when a reproducible source or bounded sample supports the number.

## Market Comparison

- Keep a global or Korean baseline separate from each requested market.
- Explain what is shared, what is market-specific, and what evidence is missing.
- Distinguish language differences from real positioning differences.
- Translate findings into the user's business question: brand fit, campaign concept, media angle,
  partnership route, live opportunity, or proposal risk.

## Output

Use the smallest structure that preserves the evidence:

1. Decision summary
2. Coverage by market and platform
3. Recurring image map with representative links
4. Keyword map with original terms, translations, and contexts
5. Market differences
6. Commercial opportunities and risks
7. Evidence gaps and source list with access dates

State whether every claim is confirmed, a repeated observed pattern, or a working hypothesis.
Never hide an inaccessible platform or substitute another market without disclosure.

## Boundaries

- Research is read-only unless the user separately approves an external action.
- Never post, comment, react, follow, connect, message, or change account settings.
- Never print, copy, or store cookies, tokens, credentials, or browser-session data.
- Do not bypass access controls, CAPTCHAs, platform limits, or account restrictions.
- Prefer current direct evidence and cite it near the finding it supports.
